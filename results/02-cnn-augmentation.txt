SkinLesionCNN with augmentation
===============================

Same model, same 15 epochs, same everything as 01. The only change is that
training photos now get flipped, turned and nudged in brightness before the
network sees them.

Setup
-----
model            SkinLesionCNN, 4 conv blocks, 391,175 parameters
input            224x224 RGB
augmentation     RandomHorizontalFlip, RandomVerticalFlip,
                 RandomRotation(20), ColorJitter(0.2, 0.2, 0.1)
                 training set only, validation and test untouched
normalisation    HAM10000 training statistics
loss             CrossEntropyLoss with class weights (inverse frequency)
optimiser        Adam, lr 0.001
batch size       32
epochs           15
device           mps
split            by lesion_id, seed 42, 70/15/15
train/val/test   6986 / 1521 / 1508 images

What the numbers say
--------------------
Two of the headline numbers got worse and the one that matters got far
better. Accuracy fell by 1.7 points and validation loss rose slightly, while
missed cancers dropped from 115 to 30.

The melanoma row explains it. Melanomas called an ordinary mole fell from 24
to 7, and that is the error with real consequences, because a patient hears
that nothing is wrong. Meanwhile 27 melanomas were called akiec and 14 bkl.
The akiec confusion costs nothing in the malignant/benign answer, since akiec
counts as malignant too, so the binary recall climbs even where the seven-way
answer is wrong.

The bill comes as false alarms: 446 against 250, and 258 ordinary moles
called melanoma. For a screening tool that is the right way round, since a
false alarm sends someone to a dermatologist and a miss sends them home.

Worth noting that validation loss was still falling at epoch 15 and training
loss sits at 1.3427, well above the 1.2392 of the unaugmented run. That is
augmentation doing its job: it made the training task harder, so the network
is now underfitting and has room to keep learning. 15 epochs is no longer
enough.

df went the other way, 23.8% to 14.3% recall. With 82 training examples and
random rotations on top, there is too little signal left to hold on to.
